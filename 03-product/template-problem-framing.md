# Problem Framing — Energy Monitor

## Introduction

This document defines the main problem addressed by the **Energy Monitor** system by
identifying who is affected, what the problem is, when it occurs, its impact, how users
currently deal with it, and why it is worth solving. This analysis establishes a clear
understanding of the problem before defining the proposed solution.

## Who Has the Problem?

The problem mainly affects members of Colombian households who consume electricity and want to
understand, control, and optimize their energy consumption.

Within the system, two main types of users are identified:

**Home Member:** A person who interacts with the application to view information related to
their household's energy consumption, check reports, and receive alerts and recommendations.

**Home Owner:** A person responsible for managing a home, including registering the home,
linking measuring devices to it, and managing which members have access.

Therefore, the affected user can be characterized as:

> A person who uses electricity at home but does not have sufficiently clear, timely, and
> detailed information to understand how their consumption is distributed and to make decisions
> to reduce it.

## What Problem Do They Have?

The main problem is the lack of knowledge and control over electricity consumption within the
household.

Currently, most people mainly know the amount they have to pay on their electricity bill, but
their service provider does not provide detailed information that allows them to determine:

- Which devices consume the most energy.
- When the highest levels of consumption occur.
- What their usual consumption patterns are.
- When there is unusually high consumption.
- What actions they can take to reduce consumption.
- How current consumption compares with previous periods.

## When Does the Problem Occur?

The problem mainly occurs during the daily use of electricity at home, especially when multiple
devices are used simultaneously without knowing how much each one contributes to total
consumption.

It also occurs when:

- Devices are left on unnecessarily.
- High-consumption appliances are used without awareness of their impact.
- Unexpected increases in consumption occur.
- The household receives an electricity bill with a higher-than-expected amount.
- A member tries to determine why consumption increased from one period to another.
- A member wants to set a consumption threshold but has no data to base it on.
- A member needs to compare current consumption with historical consumption.

## What Is the Impact?

### Economic Impact

Inefficient energy consumption increases the cost of electricity bills. The household typically
only detects the problem after the provider has issued the bill — after the spending already
happened. Discovery survey data confirms this pattern: the dominant way people track
consumption today is by reading the monthly bill, not by monitoring it as it happens.

### Environmental Impact

Unnecessary energy consumption contributes to a greater environmental impact, particularly when
the energy used provides no real benefit to the household.

### Impact on Decision-Making

Households struggle to make informed decisions — such as which device to unplug or when to
shift usage — because they lack detailed, timely information about their own consumption.

Consequently, the problem can be summarized as:

> Greater difficulty in controlling electricity expenses, identifying unnecessary consumption,
> making timely decisions, and developing efficient energy consumption habits.

## How Do They Currently Solve the Problem?

Today, households rely almost entirely on the monthly electricity bill, and occasionally on
directly observing the physical meter, as their only sources of consumption information. Both
are after-the-fact and low-resolution: neither tells a household which device is responsible for
a spike, nor warns them before the spending happens. Discovery survey data shows more than half
of respondents (54.8%) do not actively monitor their consumption at all today, despite 80.6%
considering it very important — the barrier is the lack of a convenient tool, not a lack of
interest.

## Why Is It Worth Solving?

It is worth solving because giving households clearer, near-real-time information about their
electricity consumption helps them understand their own patterns, detect unusual situations
early, and act on concrete recommendations instead of generic advice.

Solving this problem generates value in different areas:

### For the Home Member / Home Owner:

- Greater knowledge of their own energy consumption.
- Ability to identify high consumption before the bill arrives.
- The ability to set consumption thresholds appropriate to their own home.
- Information that supports decision-making.
- Reduced electricity expenses through more efficient use.

### For the Household:

- Better control of overall energy consumption.
- Identification of consumption patterns across devices and time.
- Historical tracking and month-over-month comparison.
- Reinforcement of energy-saving habits over time.

### For the Environment:

- Promotion of responsible energy consumption.
- Reduction of energy waste.
- Promotion of sustainable habits at the household level.

## Summary of the Problem

> Members of Colombian households have difficulty knowing, understanding, and controlling their
> electricity consumption because they lack clear and timely information about how, when, and
> where that consumption occurs. Today, awareness depends almost entirely on the monthly
> electricity bill, which limits a household's ability to identify high consumption in time,
> determine its causes, and take corrective action. This contributes to inefficient energy use,
> higher electricity costs, and unnecessary environmental impact.
